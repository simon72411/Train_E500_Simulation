Brake delay (dead travel)

V km/h30507090100
3.0 s (E500 measured)2542587583 m

"Dead travel distance = numerical value of speed" is an exact identity: V km/h = V/3.6 m/s, × 3.6 s = V metres. The 3.6 s originates from measurements on the GE-built E200/300/400 classes with full air brake, and is retained as a conservative value versus the E500's 3.0 s.
Note: with divisor 9 (stopping) the dead travel is already absorbed — do not add it again. With divisor 8 (partial deceleration) it must be added.

Resistance model

r(V) = 0.4 + 0.006859V + 0.000334V² [kgf/tonf] Curve = 600 / R [kgf/tonf] Gradient = i [kgf/tonf] (Tunnel effects are absorbed in the calibrated coefficients)
600/R is an international convention: TRA textbook eq. 3.20 "compensated gradient ic = i + 600/r"; Open Rails / NSW track standard "grade compensation = 60/R %" — identical. The TRA value derives from Morison's formula rc = 1000μ(G+L)/2r with μ=0.22, G=1.067 m (narrow gauge), L=4.3 m.
Fig. 1 — Running resistance components. Aerodynamic drag overtakes rolling resistance at about 35 km/h and accounts for 75% of the total at 100 km/h.
Fig. 2 — Resistance / tractive effort diagram. Solid lines are total resistance at each gradient; dashed lines are tractive effort at each throttle position. Intersections give the balancing speed. Curves below the zero line mean coasting accelerates.
Fig. 3 — Coasting deceleration on level track. With F = 0, a = −r(V) × 0.0353 ÷ 1.08. Since r(V) ≥ 0.4 always holds, coasting on level track always decelerates.
International comparison of the constant term a: Davis original (1926) 2.57 → Davis modified (1970) 1.63 → ×0.85 post-1950 adjustment 1.38 → TRA textbook steel coach 1.24 → POLIMI full-scale test (2016) 0.524 → this model 0.40. The decreasing sequence follows bearing development (plain → roller → low-friction roller → angular contact). The b and c coefficients are nearly identical to the TRA textbook (0.00686 vs 0.0069; 0.000334 vs 0.000313).
Reference: Somaschini, Rocchi, Tomasini & Schito (2016), Simplified Estimation of Train Resistance Parameters: Full Scale Experimental Tests and Analysis, Proc. 3rd Int. Conf. Railway Technology, Paper 58, doi:10.4203/ccp.110.58

Starting resistance (applies only at start)

Typekgf/tonfNote
Plain (brass) bearings8.0TRA planning value
Roller-bearing coaches3.0Tested 1.9–2.5; 3 adopted for safety
Diesel-electric locomotive5.0Must also turn gears and armature
This consist, weighted3.52(96 t × 5 + 274 t × 3) ÷ 370
Falls to its minimum at about 10 km/h, after which running resistance applies. Effect on 0→30 km/h is only 0.5 s — negligible for timetable purposes.
But not negligible at starting: 10% throttle = 2523 kgf; starting at Beipu (+2.90‰) requires 2375 kgf, leaving only 148 kgf and an acceleration of 0.013 km/h/s. This is the physical basis for the practice of applying 10% first, then increasing to 15–17%.
Textbook also notes: resistance increases with standing time (oil film squeezed out) and, counter-intuitively, with higher ambient temperature (lower oil viscosity). Japanese tests on seven 40 t coaches: couplers stretched 9.56, buffered 6.53 kgf/tonf.

Mass-weighted effective gradient

g_eff = [ Σ mᵢ × (h_front,i − h_rear,i) / Lᵢ ] ÷ M × 1000 Locomotive 96 t / 21 m (linear density 4.57 t/m) Coaches 27.4 t / 20 m × 10 (1.37 t/m) — locomotive is 3.3× denser
Within one train length (221 m) of any grade change, the gradient you see is not the gradient the train experiences. About 6.7% of the line (5.3 km) shows a discrepancy exceeding 3‰.
· Just past a crest → the rear is still on the up-grade; the train will not accelerate immediately
· Just past a valley → the rear is still on the down-grade; the train will not lose speed immediately
· Most extreme case: the crest at K3+031 approaching Su'ao New — the full consist only feels the −10.10‰ after travelling 221 m, at K2+810

Not independently validated: rotational inertia factor 1.08 (textbook gives 1.06, POLIMI measured 1.039; three back-calculations gave 0.82 / 1.26 / 1.35 — too scattered to determine), high-throttle tractive effort roll-off, and the shape of the braking curve (three different shapes reproduce the same stopping distances; the data cannot distinguish them).
